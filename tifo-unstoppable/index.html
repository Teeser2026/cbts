’Til the end of time

Side ASide A33⅓ RPM

Side A

The Holmesdale

A1

Monkey Man (Palace ’til the day that I die)

Toots & the Maytals

12 November 2026 · Simon Teesdale

Cards on the table. I think the HF are one of the best things that have happened to Palace. I’m aware they aren’t everyone’s cup of tea. But they single-handedly elevated our atmosphere from (often) humdrum to (at times) electric. They gave a medium-sized South London yo-yo club a unique identity, and helped put ultra culture on the English footballing map.

They were there at Lloyds when the club’s very existence was in the balance. They were at the forefront of the legendary support at Wembley. When UEFA took Palace’s Europa League place away, they organised a protest at Selhurst. They even took a suitcase full of fake money to UEFA’s headquarters.

That tells you most of what you need to know about the HF. They weren’t a group who did things by halves. They spent weeks making displays, organising songs, painting tifos by hand, helping out in the community. They lived and breathed it.

Their own slogan was This mentality is unstoppable.

On 27 August this year, they stopped.

Nobody has told me why. Not the club, not anyone in the group. Sure, they released a statement. It was rather vague.

What follows is me, a Palace fan, putting two and two together from what’s on the public record. I might be wrong. I hope I am. But I don’t think so, and it’s been eating at me. Eating at me because I loved being part of what they did. Backing the chants, going on the marches, having a pre-match beer or two.

A2

Against All Odds

Phil Collins

Everything they achieved was against the backdrop of an English football scene that hardly assists ultra-style support. Restrictions on flags, drums and displays. Constant rows over where you can stand or whether you must sit. Away coaches being stopped. Fans arrested. Banning orders. Heavy-handed policing and stewarding. Surveillance and facial recognition cameras. As Steve Parish told The Athletic in September, “everything’s banned in England”. They fought all of it, every season, for two decades, and kept going.

I don’t believe it was the usual stuff that stopped them. They’d been fighting the usual stuff since 2005 without giving in. That mentality was meant to be unstoppable.

A3

Never Tear Us Apart

INXS

I wrote in A Tifo for an Empty Stand about why I gave up my season ticket. It wasn’t one thing. It was VAR. It was the corporatisation of the game. It was the PA drowning out the crowd, the light shows, the sense that the atmosphere was being produced rather than made. A disconnection.

The essence of what it means to be a supporter has been nibbled away at, and with it my sense that this is something I belong to rather than something I buy. A customer.

The euphoria of winning the FA Cup gave way to the perceived injustice of losing our Europa League place under UEFA’s multi-club ownership rules. This wouldn’t have happened to Man Utd or Liverpool. Nottingham Forest got it instead. Forest had previously written to UEFA seeking clarity over Palace’s ownership structure. That’s the background to everything that follows.

End of Side A. Turn the record over.

The High Court

B1

I Fought the Law

The Clash

Here’s what’s public.

  • 24 August 2025. Palace draw 1-1 with Forest at Selhurst. A banner aimed at Forest’s owner, Evangelos Marinakis, is held up in the Holmesdale.
  • November 2025. The FA charges Palace with failing to make sure its fans behaved.
  • February 2026. Palace are fined £50,000. The FA notes the club’s own policy against banners being smuggled in, as proof the club knew the risk.
  • 10 August 2026. Marinakis issues a defamation claim in the High Court against CPFC Limited and “Persons Unknown”. It’s about two weeks before the one-year deadline for bringing it.
  • 27 August 2026. Seventeen days later, the Holmesdale Fanatics announce they are stopping “all organised activity as the HF inside the stadium”. Their statement talks about young fans being driven out by “relentless harassment and legal action”.
  • November 2026. The High Court record for the case (KB-2026-002909) is public. It shows the claim is still open, last updated on 28 October. Marinakis is represented by Slateford, and Palace by Carter-Ruck. The fans are still listed only as “Persons Unknown”. Nobody has been named.
B2

I Heard It Through the Grapevine

Marvin Gaye

Yes, I know the rumours. I know the official line. This is me putting two and two together from what’s official. I realise the answer I’m getting might not be four.

Twenty-one years of restrictions didn’t stop these lads. What’s new in the summer of 2026 is a court case in which the club can be sued over what its fans hold up, and in which the people who held it up are named only as “Persons Unknown”. Bans and restrictions hit individuals, but they’re survivable. You miss games, the group closes ranks and carries on. A defamation claim is different. It’s aimed at specific people, and it can come with damages and legal costs running to sums most fans simply don’t have.

Now think about it from the club’s perspective. The question at the heart of the claim is whether a club can be treated as the publisher of what its fans hold up. The club and the HF were hardly strangers. They wrangled over many issues. At the same time the club, in Parish’s own words, had “big battles on their behalf” and “used them in our marketing”. The closer the club is to the ultras, the harder it becomes to say a banner was nothing to do with them. It’s more than feasible that the club’s lawyers, with a defamation claim on the table, are advising the club to distance itself from those displaying provocative material. Will the club be asked to help identify “Persons Unknown”? Seat numbers, CCTV and all that jazz.

Asked about the Fanatics by The Athletic in September, Steve Parish said: “I don’t really know what’s at the heart of it.” When I spoke to Sharon Lacey earlier this month, she told me the same thing. She didn’t know exactly why they’d stopped.

I can’t prove the court case is why the HF stopped. But I know these are not people who walk away because they can’t be arsed anymore. Something made carrying on impossible, and this is the only thing I can see that’s big enough.

B3

The Way It Is

Bruce Hornsby and the Range

I understand there’s a legal argument here, and I’m not a lawyer. I’m not going to argue the case.

But this is football. For a hundred years, every ground and every terrace has sung songs and held up banners that were rude, cruel, unfair and untrue about owners, managers, players and referees. OK, it’s often true about refs. That’s not a defence of every word ever sung. It’s a description of what a football crowd is. Mockery of the powerful is part of the deal. If you own a football club, you get sung about.

There is a difference between an insult and an accusation, and the courts will decide on which side of that line this falls. I accept that. What worries me is what happens either way. If a club can be held responsible for every banner in its stands, every club in England has a reason to vet every flag, search every bag and treat every organised group of fans as a legal risk. The safest stand is one where nobody brings anything at all. One filled with tourists instead of ultras.

Billionaire owners take people to court. They can afford defamation cases. They can afford to lose them. The fans that chant and hold up banners can’t afford to be named.

B4

The End

The Doors

So here’s the loop, as I see it. Football’s ownership rules took our European place and handed it to Forest. Fans protested the man who benefited. He sued the club and the fans. The club now has to police its own supporters to protect itself. And the group the club and the league happily sold to the world as the Selhurst atmosphere has gone quiet.

The corporate game didn’t just nibble at the edges this time. It took a bite out of itself.

I’m sad about it. Gutted. Selhurst without the HF is subdued. I don’t think anyone, including those who run the club, wanted that.

Many of the HF may still be watching from the stand. But the stand has lost its unstoppable mentality.

As I write, the case is still open. One of the defendants is still “Persons Unknown”.

I hope it stays that way.

No hearing date has been listed yet. If one is, the case file opens up, and we’ll find out a lot more. Palace aren’t taking it lightly, either. They’ve gone to Carter-Ruck, one of the best-known libel firms in the country. Not a high-street solicitor in Streatham.

Whatever the outcome of the case, the club may consider that having the HF in the stand will be too great a risk.

I fear they’re gone. Never forgotten.
